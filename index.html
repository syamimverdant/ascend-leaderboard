🔋 Battery Notes: HV vs LV

Full reference guide — scroll down for all sections

1. Function of Solar PV Batteries

What it does: Stores excess solar energy during the day for use at night, during peak hours, or during blackouts.

Why customers need it:

• Energy Independence — reduce TNB reliance

• Backup Power — lights, fridge, WiFi during blackouts

• Maximise Savings — use stored energy at full value instead of selling to grid at lower rate

• Peak Shaving — use battery during expensive hours

• Future-Proofing — as costs rise, stored energy = control

💡 Pitch: "A solar system without a battery is like a water tank without a tap at night."

How it works:

1. Daytime → panels power home → excess charges battery

2. Battery full → excess goes to grid (NEM)

3. Night → battery discharges to power home

4. Battery empty → draws from grid

5. Blackout → battery provides backup (if EPS configured)

The hybrid inverter manages all this automatically.

2. Types of Solar PV Batteries

P = V × I → same power at higher voltage = less current = less loss

Example: 5kW at 400V = 12.5A | 5kW at 48V = 104A

4. Advantages & Disadvantages

HV Advantages:

✅ Faster charge & discharge

⚠️ Needs compatible hybrid inverter

✅ Easier handling (lower voltage)

⚠️ Thicker cabling

⚠️ Slower response

⚠️ Shorter lifespan

☀️ Solar PV Panels Notes

Full reference guide — scroll down for all sections

1. Function of Solar PV Panels

What it does: Converts sunlight directly into electricity. The primary energy-generating component of any solar system.

How it works:

1. Sunlight hits silicon cells → electrons knocked loose → DC electricity generated

2. Inverter converts DC → AC (usable power)

3. Home uses the power, excess charges battery or exports to grid

💡 Key Numbers:
• Peak sun hours (Malaysia): 3.4 hrs/day
• 10kW system → ~34 kWh/day
• Verdant uses 630Wp panels
• N-type panels: 30-year performance warranty

Daily Energy = System Size × 3.4 hrs × 85% efficiency

Example: 10kW → 10 × 3.4 × 0.85 = 28.9 kWh/day

3. Key Comparisons

Why TOPCon matters in Malaysia (hot climate):

At 45°C above STC (typical MY rooftop):

• PERC loses: 15.75% output

• TOPCon loses: 13.5% output

→ TOPCon generates more on every hot day, all year round.

4. Advantages & Disadvantages

Monocrystalline (Our Standard):

✅ Highest efficiency (more power per m²)

✅ Better heat performance

✅ Sleek black appearance

✅ 25–30 year lifespan

⚠️ Slightly higher cost (worth it for lifetime output)

TOPCon N-Type (Next Gen):

✅ 22–24% efficiency

✅ Lower degradation (0.4%/yr)

✅ Better in Malaysia's heat (-0.30%/°C)

✅ No first-year LID loss

✅ 30-year performance warranty

⚠️ Slightly higher cost than PERC (offset by lifetime gains)

Bifacial:

✅ Extra 5–30% energy from rear side

✅ Glass-glass = more durable

⚠️ Gain depends on surface below (dark roof = less benefit)

⚠️ Slightly heavier

💬 Customer Pitch: "Our 630W panels use the latest N-type monocrystalline technology with half-cut cells. They're designed to handle Malaysia's heat with minimal output loss, and come with a 30-year performance warranty — so you get more power per panel, every day, for decades."

If competitor uses cheaper PERC/poly panels — explain: lower upfront = lower lifetime output. Premium panels today = more savings over 30 years.

⚡ Solar Inverters Notes

Full reference guide — scroll down for all sections

1. What is a Solar Inverter?

The brain of the solar system. Converts DC (from panels) into AC (for your home).

What it does:

• DC → AC conversion (230V, 50Hz)

• MPPT — optimises panel output in real-time

• Grid synchronisation

• System monitoring (app/cloud)

• Safety protection (anti-islanding)

• Battery management (hybrid only)

💡 Analogy: Panels = engine. Inverter = gearbox. It decides how power is delivered — to home, battery, or grid.

2. Types of Inverters

StringHybridMicro

Battery❌✅❌
Backup❌✅❌
Shade HandlingWeakest linkPer-MPPT stringEach panel independent
Cost$$$$$$

MaintenanceEasyEasyHarder (on roof)
BrandsSolis, HuaweiFox ESS, Solis, HuaweiHoymiles, Enphase
💡 Verdant uses Hybrid Inverters — the all-in-one solution for solar + battery + backup.

3. When to Use Each

Hybrid Inverter (Our Standard):

• Customer wants battery or backup (now or future)

• Wants full energy management

• Frequent power outages area

• Wants to maximise self-consumption

• Not sure about battery yet → hybrid is future-proof

String Inverter:

• Cheapest solar-only, no battery ever

• Simple roof, no shading, budget priority

⚠️ If customer later wants battery → must replace entire inverter (pay twice)

Micro Inverter:

• Severe shading (trees, buildings)

• Multiple roof orientations

• Very small systems (2–4 panels)

⚠️ No battery/backup. Higher cost. Harder to service.

Decision Flowchart:
Want battery/backup? → Hybrid ✅
No battery, severe shading? → Micro
No battery, simple roof, budget? → String
Not sure? → Hybrid (future-proof) ✅

4. Advantages & Disadvantages

Hybrid Inverter (Verdant's Standard):

✅ All-in-one (solar + battery + grid + backup)

✅ Battery-ready (add later without replacing)

✅ Backup power during blackouts

✅ Smart energy management (auto-optimises)

✅ Maximise self-consumption (store for night use)

✅ Future-proof

✅ Full monitoring (solar, battery, grid, consumption)

⚠️ Higher cost than string (but far more value)

⚠️ Needs battery for backup to work

String Inverter:

✅ Lowest cost

✅ Simple, proven

✅ High efficiency (97–98%+)

⚠️ No battery support

⚠️ No backup power

⚠️ Shading drags whole string down

⚠️ Not future-proof

Micro Inverter:

✅ Best shade tolerance (panel-independent)

✅ Panel-level monitoring

✅ No high-voltage DC on roof (safer)

⚠️ Most expensive (1kWac = up to 2 panels per Mi, 2kWac = up to 4 panels per Mi)

⚠️ No battery/backup

⚠️ On roof = harder to service

⚠️ More failure points (20 panels = 5–10 micro inverters)

🔌 EPS Box vs Critical Load Rewiring

Know The Breaker. Get It Right. Every Time.

THE ONE RULE

Check the MAIN INCOMING BREAKER rating at customer's house:

✅ 63A or below = USE EPS BOX (no change)

❌ Above 63A = DO CRITICAL LOAD REWIRING (no EPS Box)

NOT the inverter model. NOT the system size. Just the breaker.

WHY NOT EPS BOX FOR >63A?

• EPS Box max input = 63A per phase. House breaker 100A = can't handle it.

• Even if forced, you'd still rewire to stay within 32A. Box becomes useless.

Result: Skip the box. Just do rewiring. Same outcome.

3 STEPS

Step 1: Take CLEAR photo of main breaker (ampere number must be readable). Upload to SSR.

⚠️ Blurry photo = delivery chain stuck!

Step 2: Read the number:

• ≤63A → SSR = 'EPS BOX'. Done.

• >63A → SSR = 'Critical Load Rewiring'. Create ticket.

Step 3: Fill SSR correctly. Never write 'EPS BOX' if breaker >63A. Attach photo as proof.

COST — WHO PAYS?

Verdant covers rewiring up to RM1,500 or 32A. FREE for customer.

• Standard rewiring (up to 32A) = Verdant pays

• Customer wants MORE than 32A = top up at extra cost

• Additional points: RM200–350 per point

Be upfront: standard scope is covered. Beyond = their choice.

WHAT IS CRITICAL LOAD REWIRING?

Pick customer's essential loads (lights, fans, fridge, aircond) → wire into separate backup circuit → connected to inverter.

Max load = Inverter output × 80%
P3 Pro 30kW = 40A × 80% = 32A/phase = 7,360W max
